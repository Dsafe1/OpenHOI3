# The icon

`icon.png` and `icon.ico` at the repository's root - the exe's icon, and the window's and the taskbar's - are made by
`make_icon.py` from `battleships.webp`: the leading ship of a line of capital ships, HMS *Repulse*, bow on, cut square and coloured as at
sunset (an orange sky, a copper sea, the ships dark against them), with rounded corners. Made again with

    python tools/icon/make_icon.py .

from the repository's root. The colouring is the script's own, by hand: the sky, the sea and the ships each given a hue,
the photograph's light and shade kept.

## The photograph

`battleships.webp`, 800 x 641, black and white, a smaller copy of
[File:Renown-7.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Renown-7.jpg) on Wikimedia Commons (5662 x 4561): "The
battlecruiser HMS *Repulse* leading other Royal Navy capital ships during maneuvers, circa the late 1920s. The next ship
astern is HMS *Renown*." Author Underwood & Underwood; source "Official U.S. Navy photo [NH 57183] from the U.S. Navy Naval
History and Heritage Command". Found on 2026-11-13.

**Licence, as the page gives it:** public domain in the United States as a work of the U.S. Navy, with a Creative Commons
Public Domain Mark 1.0. The page does not claim public domain worldwide, and its two lines sit oddly together - a Navy
employee's work, by a commercial studio. Two things hold whatever the reason (ours, not the page's): published in the
1920s, it is public domain in the United States by age; and outside it, a photograph of the 1920s by an unnamed studio
photographer is beyond the terms most countries give such works. Checked against the page on 2026-11-13.

It is not taken from an install, so the rule that nothing of an install's is committed does not touch it. The icon was also
made the one exception to that rule (2026-11-13), should it ever be made from a loading screen instead;
nothing else is excepted. The loading screen of a tank is a still from the film *Fury* (2014) and is not to be used.
